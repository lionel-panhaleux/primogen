# Procedure: a deck analysis

The bar, from the owner's grading: module-internal synergy, the deck dated
to its year, the intent of the copy counts, weaknesses tied to a posture.
"Accurate but shallow" fails: naming the modules is the start, the analysis
is why these cards, in these numbers, in that year. Theory and vocabulary:
`theory.md`, `modules.md`.

1. **Take the facts primogen computes**: the list validated, the crypt
   (groups, capacities, disciplines inferior and superior, titles), the
   library by type and module, densities and hand expectation, the date,
   event size and format. Never recompute what was computed, and never
   assume a card's text or requirements: they are in the evidence. Read the
   requirements as well as the text: an ally's recruit requirement is not in
   its ability text.
2. **Crypt first.** What does it cohere around? Star (3 or 4×), core (2×),
   support (1×). Titles count twice: as votes and as the titled reactions
   they unlock.
3. **The library as modules.** For each: density, expectation per hand,
   pillar. Find the engine (the card that grants the effect, not its
   enablers) and the turn loop: what does a mid-game turn look like, action
   by action, reactions included?
4. **The four pillars.** Payload size and reach (bounce-proof? limited by
   the rules?), delivery, defense (wakes, bounce count, bloat rate against
   expected pressure), combat posture. Deliberate absences are choices.
5. **Date it.** What did each card do then (the history of card and rules
   changes)? What did the field look like that year: with primogen's numbers
   when it gives them, as an impression otherwise. Which inclusions are meta
   calls, and would the list need adapting today?
6. **Its relatives.** The nearest archetype and tournament decks: is this
   list standard, or a deviation? Deviations are where the player's read
   lives. When nothing fits, say so and read the deck on its own mechanics;
   lineage must never become the spine of the analysis.
7. **Verdict.** Strengths; weaknesses, each naming the matchup and the
   mechanism; what the pilot must do well to win (lunge timing, deals, jam
   management).
