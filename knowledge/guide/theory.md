# Theory of the game

The frame every deck reading, piece of advice and strategy answer hangs on.
The rules facts below are the rulebook's; the rest is strategy, owner-graded.

## Economy

- **Pool is life and currency.** Each Methuselah starts with 30; ousting your
  prey gives 1 victory point and 6 pool. The first question of any deck: how
  does it remove its prey's 30 and more, and how fast? The second: what does
  it do on the turn it cannot?
- **Bloat is generic defense**: pool gained is damage undone. A leech package
  (Villein, Minion Tap) turns minions' blood into pool, then refills them.
  Minion Tap moves any amount, Villein 2 to 5: Tap is the leech for big
  capacities.

## The four pillars

**Payload** (bleed, votes, damage permanents), **delivery** (stealth, block
denial, swarm width, vote lock), **defense** (bloat, bounce, blocks, wakes),
**combat management** (disabling, grinding or resisting). Also the order in
which single copies are cut.

- **Bounce is the backbone of bleed defense and a tempo weapon**: a bounced
  bleed spends the predator's power on the prey. A deck with reliable bounce
  does not open by rushing backward; it spends its aggression disabling its
  prey's defense.
- **Acting and blocking on every turn is table stakes**, not a finding. Flag
  the exceptions: all-forward builds, bloat as the sole defense.
- **Bloat as the sole defense is a deliberate variant.** Its signature: no
  wakes, no bounce, heavy leech with a refill loop (Minion Tap ×7 and Voter
  Captivation ×8: tap to 2 or 3 blood, refill on passed referendums, tap
  again). Its Strike: combat ends cards are there to be cycled, not to hold
  a line.

## Card flow

- A hand is 7 cards: a module's expectation per hand is its density × 7.
- Density classes: spam above 25%, strong 14 to 25%, standard 7 to 14%,
  tactical below 7%. Deck size is about the peak rotation × 12 turns.
- Situational modules jam; look for the release valves (Dreams of the Sphinx,
  The Barrens, cards with two modes). Recursion beats rotation.

## Copy counts encode intent

Weight attention by how often a card is drawn, not by how clever it is.

- 5× Information Highway with 7× Minion Tap plans 3 or 4 big vampires, not 2.
- A doubled unique must land early. Doubled permanents are near-certain to be
  drawn and mark deliberate structure.
- A single copy beside a fetch engine is a searchable answer. A bare single
  copy is an opportunity (a lunge tool, a situational answer), never a core
  piece.
- A single crypt card is texture, not a role, unless something fetches it:
  you open without it about two games in three. Only the 3 or 4× star is a
  role.
- A single anti-jam card (The Barrens beside Dreams of the Sphinx) marks a
  deliberately tight module.
- No wakes is a deliberate all-forward choice.

## The free verbs

The rules give every deck a layer that costs no card; card slots only make
sense against it.

- **The Edge is an economy.** Its holder may gain 1 pool in each of their
  unlock phases, may burn it for 1 vote, and some cards spend it (Inside
  Dirt). Any successful bleed gives it to the acting minion's controller,
  a redirected bleed included. Track who holds it, who can take it, and
  whether a deck is built to keep it or to spend it.
- **Torpor is a cost, not an endgame.** Leaving torpor is an action (2 blood,
  +1 stealth); a rescue costs 2 blood, split freely between rescuer and
  rescued; hunting gains 1 blood. A deck with thick blood regeneration (Jake
  Washington, The Coven) and no combat defense reads correctly: the
  regeneration is its combat defense.
- **Every ready vampire bleeds for 1.** Width is a clock with no card in
  hand; bouncing a bleed for 1 is a losing trade, and clearing weak bodies is
  never free for the prey.
- **Hunting prices blood in actions**, one action per blood. Better hunts and
  leech loops change that rate; that is their real value.
- **A blocked action always leads to combat** (leaving torpor aside). A
  0-strength ally blocking a bleed still enters combat and survives on its
  life, which is why body count and life (Vagabond Mystic) carry a wall. A small blocker is
  never "damage-free".
- **The base actions are always available enablers.** Becoming Anarch costs 2
  blood, or 1 with another ready Anarch: a trigger that "nothing in the deck
  fires" may be fired by a base action.

## Engines and pieces

The card that grants the effect is the engine; the cards that enable it are
pieces. Madness Network and Enkil Cog grant actions in other Methuselahs'
minion phases: engines. Metro Underground only unlocks: a piece, necessary
and good support. Attribute a combo's power to the engine.

## Archetypes and their matchups

- **Bleed**: the most damage to the prey, weak in combat, bounce or bloat
  behind.
- **Vote**: heavy damage and bloat once locked, weak in combat and against
  bleed. Choosing where the damage goes is table control, the real reason to
  play politics.
- **Wall**: exceptional defense, weak offense, wins long games, protects
  fragile payload permanents.
- **Toolbox**: versatile, must play its hand.
- **Rush**: table control, not a win plan. It removes bouncers, blockers or a
  menace, but pool damage must still come from somewhere: identify the
  removal and the clock (Fame, Dragonbound and the like convert torpor into
  pool loss).
- **Combo**: absolute advantage, fragile, draws the table's hate.

About 40% of tournament-winning decks match no named archetype. Read the
mechanics first; a name is one line of orientation, dropped as soon as the
engine diverges.

## Politics

- A Kine Resources Contested allocates 4 points among two or more
  Methuselahs; the default split is 3 on the prey and 1 on the predator. Hop
  to the grand-prey when the prey dies this turn anyway. Sometimes pay a
  cross-table player, even a victory point, to avoid sitting next to an
  unmanageable deck: combat and wall decks are worth removing early.
- Votes where each Methuselah burns 1 pool per minion they control (Anarchist
  Uprising, Ancilla Empowerment) are also among the largest total pool
  removals in the game; they advance the game with no swarm on the table.
- Allies with lasting intercept (The Unmasking with Carlton Van Wyk,
  Ponticulus, legionnaires) force stealth onto votes too. Superior Force of
  Personality (vampires burn 1 blood to block, non-zombie allies cannot) is
  targeted tech against exactly this.

## Combat

Combat is not the default focus: payload, delivery, defense and economy come
first. Read it deeply only in a combat deck, and then for its internal
synergy, not its card list: what sets up what, which range and aim cards
pair, what the finishers need, what sustains the grind.

- **A weakness is posture-aware.** Strike: combat ends matters only if the
  plan must win combats; a wall of it as prey is a real matchup problem, the
  same card in a non-wall is not.
- **A combat line is tested against the defender's standard answers.** A
  "combat ends" strike resolves before every other strike, first strike
  included; Immortal Grapple leaves only hand strikes for the round.
  Telepathic Tracking is the usual answer to the first.
- **By default, one strike per minion per round**: without a card granting
  an additional strike, two strike cards compete for it.
- **Swarm attrition answers "combat ends"**: cheap bodies in series eat the
  dodges until a strike lands. Do not score a swarm by one-on-one exchanges.
- **Check who wields a package without disciplines.** Only a vampire meeting
  a card's discipline, clan or blood cost can play it, so allies play the
  cards that have none: a disciplineless weapon suite may arm the allies.
- **A little stealth changes a lunge.** One or two points (Power of One, An
  Anarch Manifesto) mean the prey needs real intercept, not bodies.

## Table dynamics

Cross-table players are natural allies. Pressure ripples along the
predator-prey chain. Timing the lunge is the key skill: a
failed lunge invites the table onto you. On all of it sit deals, standing
management and lying low.
