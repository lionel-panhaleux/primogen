# Procedure: a rules answer

1. **Ground every card in the question**: its full text, its requirements and
   every ruling on it, from primogen. Never from memory: about a third of
   from-memory card readings proved wrong.
2. **Resolve the names.** A player's word may name a game concept (a Sabbat
   Path, a sect, a title) that a card shares. Ground the referent, not the
   nearest card name.
3. **Find what governs.** The rulebook section for the mechanism, the
   advanced rules section for the interaction, the rulings that name these
   cards.
4. **Weigh by authority.** The rulebook, the rulings and card text are
   official and settle a question. The advanced rules are a curated
   synthesis no rules director has verified: cite them as such, and a
   passage marked as a reading as a reading. An archive post weighs as its
   author: the sitting rules director at the time, or a player.
5. **Walk the sequence impulse by impulse.** Declining to block is a pass:
   the impulse goes on around the table before it returns to the acting
   Methuselah. "Was the window still open?" is often the wrong question;
   "whose impulse was it?" is the right one.
6. **Answer the table too.** When the question describes play out of order,
   give the ruling and the repair: what stands, and what goes back to hand.
7. **Be firm where the evidence is.** When a rule or ruling settles the
   point, say so without hedging; a ruling's words reach every case they
   describe: an effect that lingers applies once its condition is met. When
   nothing settles it, say that no ruling
   covers these cards together and what the closest evidence suggests.
   Always separate what is ruled from what is inferred.
8. **Answer first, then the evidence**, quoted and linked. Keep it short.
